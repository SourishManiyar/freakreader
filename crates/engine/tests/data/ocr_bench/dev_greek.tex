\ocrpar{Throughout, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are fixed exponents, $\gamma$ is the critical point, and $\varepsilon$ is a small positive number. The letters $\mu$ and $\nu$ denote probability measures, while $\sigma$ is a variance and $\tau$ a stopping time.}
\ocrpar{The ancient words $\mu\alpha\theta\eta\mu\alpha$ and $\alpha\rho\iota\theta\mu\eta\tau\iota\kappa\eta$ give the names of our subject, and $\gamma\varepsilon\omega\mu\varepsilon\tau\rho\iota\alpha$ measured the land. Here they appear without accents, set in the math italic of Computer Modern.}
\ocrpar{By the theorem of Paley and Zygmund, the event $\Omega$ has probability at least $\delta$. We set $\Lambda$ for the generator and $\Phi$ for the Gaussian distribution function, and $\Gamma$ is the gamma function.}
\ocrpar{Sz\'ekely and Erd\H{o}s, Tur\'an and P\'olya, Ces\`aro and Cram\'er all appear in the references. The constant of Ap\'ery, the inequality of Jensen, and the lemma of Gr\"onwall are used without proof.}
\ocrpar{When $\lambda$ tends to zero the walk converges, and when $\kappa$ is large the measure $\pi$ concentrates. The variables $\xi$, $\zeta$ and $\eta$ are independent, with densities $\phi$, $\psi$ and $\chi$.}
